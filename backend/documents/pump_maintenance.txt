CENTRIFUGAL PUMP MAINTENANCE GUIDE

Centrifugal pumps should be regularly inspected for abnormal vibration, noise, temperature, leakage, pressure variation, and reduced flow.

Common causes of abnormal centrifugal pump operation include bearing wear, shaft misalignment, impeller imbalance, cavitation, inadequate lubrication, suction blockage, and loose foundations.

High vibration in a centrifugal pump should be investigated immediately. The maintenance team should inspect bearings, shaft alignment, impeller condition, coupling condition, foundation bolts, and lubrication.

Low discharge pressure can result from insufficient pump speed, incorrect impeller size, clogged suction line, inadequate suction pressure, impeller damage, excessive system resistance, or cavitation.

Reduced flow rate can be caused by suction blockage, clogged filters, partially closed valves, impeller problems, incorrect pump speed, or cavitation.

Pump bearings should be monitored for abnormal temperature, noise, and vibration. Excessive bearing temperature may indicate insufficient lubrication, excessive lubrication, bearing wear, shaft misalignment, or excessive mechanical loading.

Preventive maintenance should include vibration monitoring, bearing inspection, lubrication checks, coupling inspection, shaft alignment verification, and inspection of the impeller and casing.
