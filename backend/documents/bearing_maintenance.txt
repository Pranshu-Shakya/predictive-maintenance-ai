BEARING FAILURE AND MAINTENANCE

Bearings support rotating shafts and allow relative motion while
carrying radial and/or axial loads. In centrifugal pumps, bearing
condition is important because bearing deterioration can produce
increased vibration, temperature rise, noise and eventually shaft
or rotating assembly damage.

COMMON SYMPTOMS OF BEARING PROBLEMS

Common symptoms include:

- Increased vibration
- Abnormally high bearing temperature
- Unusual noise
- Lubricant leakage or deterioration
- Excessive bearing clearance
- Rough or irregular rotation

COMMON CAUSES

Possible causes include:

- Insufficient lubrication
- Excessive or incorrect lubrication
- Contaminated lubricant
- Bearing wear
- Improper bearing installation
- Excessive loading
- Shaft misalignment
- Unbalanced rotating components
- Contamination by dust, water or other particles

BEARING INSPECTION

During maintenance, inspect:

- Bearing raceways
- Rolling elements
- Cage condition
- Lubricant condition
- Signs of overheating
- Pitting or spalling
- Discoloration
- Excessive clearance
- Evidence of contamination

MAINTENANCE ACTIONS

Depending on the observed condition:

- Verify correct lubrication.
- Replace contaminated or degraded lubricant according to
  equipment requirements.
- Inspect bearing surfaces for wear or damage.
- Check shaft and housing condition.
- Check alignment of connected rotating components.
- Replace damaged bearings using the correct installation method.

SAFETY

Before inspecting or replacing a bearing, isolate the equipment
according to the applicable maintenance and lockout procedures.
Allow hot components to cool before inspection where applicable.

IMPORTANT

Vibration and temperature measurements should be interpreted
together with equipment history, operating conditions and
manufacturer specifications. A single sensor reading should not
be treated as definitive proof of bearing failure.
