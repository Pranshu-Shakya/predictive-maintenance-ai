PUMP CAVITATION AND TROUBLESHOOTING

Cavitation is a hydraulic phenomenon in which local liquid pressure
falls sufficiently low for vapor bubbles to form. When these bubbles
move into regions of higher pressure, they collapse. Repeated bubble
formation and collapse can produce noise, vibration and damage to
pump components.

COMMON SYMPTOMS

Typical symptoms include:

- Irregular or increased vibration
- Crackling or rattling noise
- Reduced pump performance
- Reduced flow rate
- Fluctuating pressure
- Damage or pitting on impeller surfaces
- Unstable pump operation

COMMON CAUSES

Possible causes include:

- Insufficient suction pressure
- Excessive suction lift
- Restricted suction line
- Clogged suction strainer
- Excessive liquid temperature
- Insufficient NPSH available
- Pump operating outside its intended operating range
- Excessive flow demand

NPSH

Net Positive Suction Head is an important parameter when evaluating
pump cavitation. Adequate suction conditions must be maintained so
that the available NPSH is sufficient for the pump operating
condition.

INSPECTION

When cavitation is suspected:

- Check suction pressure.
- Check suction piping for restrictions.
- Inspect suction strainers.
- Check liquid temperature.
- Check flow rate.
- Check operating point against the pump operating range.
- Inspect the impeller for erosion or pitting.
- Review available NPSH against pump requirements.

CORRECTIVE ACTIONS

Possible actions include:

- Remove suction-side restrictions.
- Correct abnormal suction conditions.
- Reduce excessive flow demand where appropriate.
- Check liquid temperature.
- Verify pump operating conditions.
- Correct piping or system problems causing inadequate suction
  conditions.
- Inspect damaged rotating components.

SAFETY

Pump isolation and applicable pressure-relief procedures should be
followed before opening or inspecting the pump or connected piping.

IMPORTANT

Cavitation should not be diagnosed from vibration alone. Suction
pressure, flow rate, liquid temperature, pump operating point and
system conditions should also be considered.
